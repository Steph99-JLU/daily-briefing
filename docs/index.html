US export controls cut NSA access to Anthropic's Mythos vulnerability scanner

The NSA lost operational access to Anthropic's Mythos AI-powered security tool after the US government tightened export-control classification around advanced AI models. Mythos had been used for automated vulnerability research; it now falls under restrictions that prevent certain federal deployments. A separate community benchmarking effort found that no current open model consistently matches Mythos on confirmed vulnerabilities — meaning the restriction forecloses a capability with no near-term substitute in the public sector.

The structural contradiction: the tool the US security apparatus most needs to defend critical infrastructure may be legally unavailable to it — while adversaries face no equivalent restriction.

Prompt injection via role confusion: 61% jailbreak rate before fix

Researchers at Gray Swan and Simon Willison independently documented a prompt injection class that works by disrupting a model's role identity — causing it to follow injected instructions rather than the user's. Pre-fix success rate was 61% across several major models. Anthropic and others applied "destyling" countermeasures. The vector is relevant for any pipeline that ingests external data (documents, emails, web content) and passes it to an agent with tool access.

Inference is a zero-margin trap — unless you escape cost-plus pricing

🔭 Analyst Take
  • Anthropic is vertically integrating. Series H with Micron as equity investor, a dedicated memory supply agreement, Claude Tag for enterprise Slack, and Mythos for government security work — Anthropic is building relationships across the stack that reduce dependence on OpenAI-adjacent hyperscalers and position Claude as embedded infrastructure rather than a commodity API.
  • Hyperscalers are losing the compute narrative. Both the Reflection AI/SpaceX deal and Anthropic's Micron agreement show that frontier labs now have sufficient market power to lock up dedicated infrastructure outside AWS, Azure, and GCP. Revenue growth assumptions for the three major cloud providers should be stress-tested against this trend.
  • AI security is bifurcating — offence is ahead. OpenAI Daybreak and the Five Eyes warning landed on the same day; the timing was not coincidental. Adversaries are already integrating AI into offensive operations. The enterprise security apparatus is still in procurement debates. The gap between offensive AI capability and defensive AI tooling is widening.
  • Agentic supply chain attacks are the next primary vector. OpenClaw (malicious agent skills), GitHub Actions pwn requests, and the LastPass Klue incident all appeared in the same news cycle. The pattern is consistent: as AI agents receive broader system permissions, the supply chain of skills, plugins, and tools they ingest becomes a primary attack surface. Audit everything that has write permissions.
  • The RWA credit thesis is failing structurally. MainStreet Finance, Alturax, and now Goldfinch — undercollateralised on-chain lending to emerging markets collapses when defaults exceed off-chain enforcement capacity. The Goldfinch analysis in TLDR Crypto proposes a composite standard: proof of solvency plus legal-claim attestation, not just proof of reserves. Until that standard exists, the category warrants scepticism.
